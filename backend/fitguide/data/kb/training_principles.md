# Training principles

## Progressive overload
To keep improving, gradually ask more of your muscles: add a rep, add a small amount of weight, add a set or slow the tempo. Double progression works well: pick a rep range such as 8 to 12 and add weight only after you hit the top of the range on every set.

## Frequency
Training each muscle twice a week works well for most people. Full-body plans do this with three days, upper/lower with four days and push/pull/legs with five or six.

## Rest between sets
Rest one to two minutes for isolation exercises and two to three minutes for heavy compound lifts like squats and deadlifts.

## Effort
Leave one to three reps in reserve (RIR) on most sets. Use rating of perceived exertion: RPE 7 means about three reps left, RPE 9 means about one rep left.

## Technique first
Learn the movement with light weight, then add load. Stop a set when your form breaks down.

## Deloads
Every 6 to 10 weeks, or when you feel run down for several days, cut volume by about 40 to 50 percent for a week.

## Levels
Beginner: under about 6 months of consistent training. Intermediate: 6 months to 2 years. Advanced: more than 2 years with solid technique and steady progress.
