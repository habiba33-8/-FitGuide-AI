# Workout plan approaches

## Full body (2 to 3 days per week)
Each session trains the whole body: a squat or hinge, a push, a pull, and core. Best for beginners and busy people because every muscle is trained several times a week. Rest at least one day between sessions.

## Upper / Lower (4 days per week)
Two upper-body days and two lower-body days. Each muscle is trained twice a week. Good for beginners moving to intermediate and for people with limited time.

## Push / Pull / Legs (5 to 6 days per week)
Push day: chest, shoulders, triceps. Pull day: back and biceps. Leg day: quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves. Run it once for 3 days or twice for 6 days. Best for intermediate and advanced people who enjoy training often.

## Bodyweight and home (no machine)
Uses push-ups, squats, lunges, bridges, planks and bands or dumbbells. Progress by adding reps, slowing down, using harder variations or pausing at the hardest point.

## Fat loss focus
Same lifting structure as above to keep muscle, plus short daily walks or two light cardio sessions a week. Sets stay in the 8 to 15 rep range.

## Muscle gain focus
More sets for the major muscles, 6 to 12 reps for compound lifts and 10 to 15 for isolation work, a small calorie surplus and good sleep.

## How sets and reps are chosen
Beginners start with 2 to 3 sets per exercise and 8 to 12 reps. Intermediates use 3 sets and add a fourth set for key lifts. Advanced lifters use 3 to 4 sets, heavier loads and more exercise variety.
